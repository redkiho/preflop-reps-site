How we grade

Preflop Reps · the same text as Settings → How we grade in the app

Every answer comes from one of four kinds of source, and the app says which. Nothing is graded on a hunch.

Exact: arithmetic, no opinion

  • Hand against hand. Equities are exact for all 169 × 169 starting hands, counted over every possible board. Ace-king against queens is 43.24%; aces against kings is 81.95%.
  • Pot odds and outs. The share of the pot you need comes straight from the bet, and a draw's chance comes from counting every turn and river card (all 990 runouts on the flop).

Solved (Nash): short stacks

  • The tournament shove-or-fold and call-a-shove charts are Nash equilibrium solutions in chips, worked out from those exact equities for 2 to 9 players, with and without antes. They reproduce the published heads-up chart.
  • Rejams at 12–30bb come from the same kind of solve, checked against an independent commercial calculator. They are labelled Nash in the app.
  • A shove-or-fold chart answers a game where those are the only two moves. From 16 big blinds up, the usual play first in is a small raise, so the drills stop asking for first-in shoves above 15bb.

Standard, three or more publishers: the cheat sheet

  • Opening from each seat, limpers, facing a raise, a raise and callers, the big blind, facing a 3-bet and the sizes are standard ranges, not solved ones.
  • A spot ships only when at least three independent publishers agree on it: similar width, with the boundary hands within about one notch. Several articles from one publisher count once. Every card lists its publishers.
  • Sizes follow the sheet's own open: 4bb live, 2.5bb online. A 3-bet and a 4-bet are counted from the open you made.

Held back: what we leave out

  • When the sources agree on part of a spot but not all of it (which hands to 3-bet, say, but not which to call), the app teaches the part they agree on. It never deals you a hand whose answer they don't.
  • The card says what it leaves to your judgement.
  • Postflop rules are rules of thumb, and the app says so; none is presented as solved.

The stars

  • Three stars: the main play.
  • Two stars: another play that is standard for that hand.
  • One star: a mistake. You see where the range stops and the size to use, a plain rule, and the chart with the boundary circled.

The chart you see is drawn from the same table that graded you, so the two cannot disagree.

What we don't claim

We don't call anything solved that isn't. Standard ranges are good defaults for a typical low-stakes live game, not the last word against every opponent. If a source changes or we find a mistake, we fix the card and say so in the release notes.

Think a hand is graded wrong? Open an issue with the position, stack and hand.
